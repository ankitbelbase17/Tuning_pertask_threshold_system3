\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Pass 1: every task yields a winner}

The pass-1 grid completed in full: 1{,}410 sample-evaluations over 94 cells, every
cell scored on its complete 15-video set. The selection rule of
Section~\ref{sec:protocol} returned a threshold for all nine tasks
(Table~\ref{tab:thresholds}). Taken at face value this is a successful per-task
fit, and Figure~\ref{fig:f1} shows the nine threshold curves it was read off.

\subsection{The fits do not survive resampling}

Table~\ref{tab:thresholds} reports, beside each selected threshold, the two
statistics defined in Section~\ref{sec:protocol}. Across 2{,}000 paired bootstrap
draws:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Every task's $\Delta$time-F1 against its best rival has a 95\,\%
      interval containing zero.} No selected threshold is distinguishable from the
      best alternative in its own grid.
\item \textbf{Re-selection rates are 25--61\,\%} against chance rates of 8--10\,\%.
      The curves are therefore not pure noise --- the argmax is not uniform over
      the grid --- but they are far from identifying a unique operating point.
\item \texttt{semantic\_condition\_alert} re-selects at \textbf{6\,\%} against an
      8\,\% chance rate: its fitted value reproduces \emph{less} often than a cell
      drawn at random, and the bootstrap prefers $0.15$ (33\,\%) over the selected
      $0.25$.
\end{itemize}

Two entries in the threshold table are not fits at all, and are reported as such
rather than carried into the final configuration:

\paragraph{\texttt{instant\_event\_alert} scores time-F1 $=0.000$ at all ten
thresholds.} Its apparent 100\,\% re-selection is a ten-way tie broken
deterministically by the selection rule, not agreement between draws. The rule
correctly flags the task \textsc{flat} and retains its shipped value. The system
never places a single emission within $\pm 3$\,s of a ground-truth event on this
task at any gate setting.

\paragraph{\texttt{explicit\_target\_grounding} alternates $0.065$, $0.000$,
$0.061$, $0.000$ across the grid.} With 15 emissions against 16 ground-truth events,
$\mathrm{F1}=2\,\mathrm{tp}/31$, so those values are \emph{one matched event, or
zero}. The whole curve is a single coin flip per cell, and its interval is
correspondingly the widest observed, $[-0.207,+0.182]$.

\subsection{Why: the gating score does not rank events}

Figure~\ref{fig:roc} reports per-task ROC for $p_{\mathrm{hit}}$ against the
$\pm 3$\,s positive label. AUC is $0.431$--$0.551$ and every 95\,\% interval
contains $0.5$. Three innocent explanations are excluded:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Not a clock offset.} Sweeping the tick clock over $\pm 10$\,s in
      2\,s steps leaves AUC within $0.39$--$0.55$ with no peak anywhere; a genuine
      misalignment would produce one.
\item \textbf{Not too tight a tolerance.} Re-running the label at $\pm 10$\,s
      leaves the result unchanged.
\item \textbf{Not a degenerate score.} $p_{\mathrm{hit}}$ takes 232--284 distinct
      values spanning $0.000$--$0.997$ with an interquartile range of $0.2$--$0.7$.
      It is well spread; it simply does not track \emph{when}.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{semantic\_condition\_alert} is below chance and falls monotonically as the
offset goes positive ($0.487$ at $-10$\,s to $0.389$ at $+10$\,s): its confidence is
systematically \emph{lower} near an event.

Figure~\ref{fig:bands} confirms this through an independent path. Per-sample
precision is flat across the entire grid on every task ($0.11$--$0.25$ depending on
task, with no trend), while recall falls monotonically. Firing less often buys no
precision --- which is what a ranking statistic at chance predicts, and which no
threshold can repair.

\subsection{The gate has one identifiable degree of freedom}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} pools all nine tasks. The fitted per-task arm scores
highest in absolute terms ($0.2544$), but its paired advantage over a single global
threshold is $+0.0075$ with a 95\,\% interval of $[-0.011,+0.027]$. This is the
\emph{in-sample} comparison: the nine thresholds were fitted on these very videos
and hold nine free parameters against the global arm's one, and they still do not
separate.

The global threshold, by contrast, is identified. Re-running the entire single-global
sweep inside every bootstrap draw, $0.15$ wins $94\,\%$ of them against a $10\,\%$
chance rate (next best $0.55$, at $2\,\%$). The shipped per-task table is
significantly \emph{worse} than a flat $0.15$ ($-0.0354$, interval excluding zero),
driven by recall: $0.356$ against $0.601$. Since the shipped gate in fact fired at a
fixed global $0.5$, moving that single operating point is the one intervention in
this study with a stable, reproducible effect.

\subsection{Held-out evaluation}

The claims above are measured on the 135-video fit subset and are therefore
in-sample for the fitted arm. The complete 2{,}700-sample evaluation, the
fit-disjoint rescore, and the per-task breakdown are \PENDING; Tables~\ref{tab:full}
and~\ref{tab:pertask} are reserved for them. We record here, in advance of that run,
the prediction that its pooled time-F1 under the fitted per-task thresholds will
land within the $0.03$ noise floor of the same run under the shipped thresholds. If
that prediction fails, the fit is real and this section is wrong.
