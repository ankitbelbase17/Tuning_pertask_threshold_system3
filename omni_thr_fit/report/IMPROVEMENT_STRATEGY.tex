\large A diagnosis of the Qwen2.5-Omni fork, and what to do next}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{The short answer.} The audio/video interleaving is \emph{not} the problem --- three
separate checks clear it. The problem is upstream of the gate: the ICL control DSL that
works on Qwen3-VL-8B \textbf{does not transfer} to Qwen2.5-Omni-7B. The controller
generates a mean of \textbf{21.7 tokens per tick against a 300-token budget}, stopping on
its own before the schema is finished; \texttt{fps} appears in \textbf{12 of 78{,}828}
ticks and \texttt{next\_check\_s} in \textbf{8}. The gate is thresholding a boolean
produced by a generation step that is not working, which is why AUC $\approx$ 0.5 and why
no threshold helped. That is a \emph{fixable upstream cause}, not a property of the task.
\end{keybox}

\section{Where we actually are}

Stage 3, arm 1 (fitted per-task thresholds), 2{,}512 of 2{,}700 samples banked, judge
offline so content metrics are \textsc{withheld}.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
stratum & $n$ & $n_{gt}$ & $n_{emit}$ & t-P / t-R & \textbf{time-F1} \\
\midrule
audio required      & 1643 & 4916 & 16185 & 0.155 / 0.510 & 0.2375 \\
audio not required  &  868 & 3511 & 12866 & 0.164 / 0.601 & 0.2578 \\
\textbf{gross}      & 2511 & 8427 & 29051 & 0.159 / 0.548 & \textbf{0.2464} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Two facts frame everything below.

\textbf{(1) The system emits 3.4$\times$ more often than there are events} (29{,}051
emissions against 8{,}427 ground-truth triggers). Precision, not recall, is the binding
constraint.

\textbf{(2) The gate is nearly exhausted.} \S2.5 measured precision as \emph{flat} across
the whole threshold grid. With $p$ pinned at 0.159, $F_1 = 2pr/(p+r)$ climbs monotonically
as $r \to 1$ to a ceiling of $2p/(p{+}1) = \mathbf{0.274}$. We are at 0.246. Every
remaining gate adjustment in existence is worth $\le 0.028$ --- \emph{inside} the 0.03
noise band --- and collecting it means emitting even more. Note the corollary, which is
counter-intuitive: cutting the 3.4$\times$ over-emission would \emph{lower} $F_1$, because
precision will not rise to compensate.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{\texttt{snapshot\_counting} isolates the timing problem.} Exactly 1.00 ground-truth
event per video, exactly 1.00 emission per video, and time-F1 0.0674 --- so the single
emission lands within $\pm$3\,s only about 7\% of the time. Per-emission timing, not
emission volume, is what fails here.
\end{keybox}

\noindent\textbf{But volume is \emph{also} a lever on these tasks, and \S5's comparison
proves it.} The parent system scores 0.2776 on \texttt{snapshot\_counting} while emitting
\textbf{2.85} times per ground-truth event, against our 1.00. Inverting $F_1 = 2\,tp/(k+1)$
for one GT event and $k$ emissions: the parent converts $\approx$53\% of videos into a hit
with 2.85 attempts ($\approx$24\% per attempt); we convert 6.7\% with one. \emph{Both}
factors are real --- our per-emission timing is roughly 3.5$\times$ worse \emph{and} we take
a third as many attempts. The 600\,s / 300\,s refractories inherited from the vision-only
fit are therefore binding on all three group-B tasks, not just
\texttt{instant\_event\_alert}.

\section{What is actually broken}

\subsection{The controller stops generating after $\sim$22 tokens}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
measurement (78{,}828 controller ticks, 4 lanes) & value \\
\midrule
\texttt{controller\_max\_tokens} budget & 300 \\
mean tokens actually generated per tick & \textbf{21.7} \ (max 108) \\
ticks emitting \texttt{seen} & 100\% \\
ticks emitting \texttt{answer} & 51\% \\
ticks emitting \texttt{event\_time\_s} & 41\% \\
ticks emitting \texttt{fps} & \textbf{12} \ (0.015\%) \\
ticks emitting \texttt{next\_check\_s} & \textbf{8} \ (0.010\%) \\
ticks emitting \texttt{question\_for\_next} & 21 \ (0.027\%) \\
\midrule
probe argmax is literally \texttt{true}/\texttt{false} & 84.5\% \\
probe argmax agrees with the parsed JSON & 77\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The budget is 300 and the model uses 22. It is not being truncated --- it \textbf{chooses
to stop}, mid-schema. Representative \texttt{seen} payloads from the live logs:

\begin{center}\small\ttfamily
"1 prices are ...' \textbackslash n * Do not other numbers" \quad\textbullet\quad
"\{a single CJK character\}" \quad\textbullet\quad "0\textbackslash n\{"
\end{center}

\noindent with \texttt{answer} fields reading \texttt{"\{"} and \texttt{"1.0,"}. The JSON
parses --- 0\% unparseable --- so nothing raises an error. This is the project's
signature failure mode: \emph{a finished run and a number you could put in a table}.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Why this explains AUC $\approx$ 0.5.} \texttt{have\_enough\_info} is the boolean the
gate thresholds. It is produced by the same degenerate generation step. A threshold on a
boolean that the model is not really computing cannot rank event-adjacent ticks above quiet
ones --- so the four independent measurements of ``no threshold helps'' were all measuring
one upstream defect, not nine hard tasks.
\end{keybox}

\subsection{The self-pacing loop is dead}

\texttt{fps} and \texttt{next\_check\_s} are how the ICL controller was supposed to
\emph{schedule itself} --- look more often when something is happening, less when it is
not. They appear in 20 ticks out of 78{,}828. The system is running at a fixed cadence with
its adaptive machinery present in the prompt and absent from the output. This is the parent
branch's defect \#7 (``fields simply never appeared'') recurring in a new form.

\subsection{A verified structural divergence: the modality delimiters}

The reference processor builds an interleaved A/V stream as

\begin{center}\small\ttfamily
\textless vision\_bos\textgreater\textless audio\_bos\textgreater\ [video chunk $j$][audio
chunk $j$]$^{*}$ \textless audio\_eos\textgreater\textless vision\_eos\textgreater
\end{center}

\noindent Our \emph{ordering} matches it: video frames for $[t,t{+}2)$, then the 2\,s audio
chunk for the same window. But \textbf{none of the four delimiter tokens appears anywhere in
\texttt{async\_omni\_v2/}}. The model is fed an interleaved stream with no marker saying an
interleaved stream has begun.

This is a real divergence from training conditions, verified from the reference source. It
is \textbf{not} established as a \emph{cause} --- no ablation has been run. It is cheap to
test and belongs high on the list precisely because it is cheap, not because it is proven.

\section{Why the original ICL branch appears to score so much higher}

The number is \texttt{joint\_f1} \textbf{0.206}, recorded in \texttt{EXPERIENCE.md} as
``roughly MiniCPM-o-4.5-level''. Its own parenthetical is the important part:
\emph{``27-video, 9-task eval, older scoring.''}

\texttt{LEARNINGS.md} \S6b then documents what ``older scoring'' meant --- six defects, all
in the scorer:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{clp{7.6cm}}
\toprule
\# & defect & effect on the number \\
\midrule
13 & LLM judge silently fell back to \textbf{word overlap} on any exception, while logging
``google-genai judge active'' & every judged number \\
17 & state matched by \textbf{substring}, not equality & ``RSM content accuracy inflated'' \\
15 & \texttt{semantic\_condition\_alert} LLM-judged; upstream scores it \texttt{time\_only}
& wrong metric entirely \\
14 & \texttt{judge\_cache.json} clobbered by 4 concurrent writers & 24\,h of judging
$\rightarrow$ 42 entries \\
16 & \texttt{\_extract\_count} did not strip clock times: ``At 01:23, count is 5'' $=1$ &
correct answers marked wrong \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Defect \#13 was caught by re-scoring in a bare shell \textbf{with no API key at all}, which
reproduced the shipped \texttt{content\_acc} \emph{to four decimals}. A real judge and no
judge cannot agree exactly --- so no judge had ever run.

Aligning to the benchmark's real scorer then found \texttt{realtime\_state\_monitor} at
\textbf{0.000, 296 of 296 wrong}: it emitted sentences where the benchmark compares the
whole payload to \texttt{state\_to} by equality. That fix (emit the bare label) is commit
\texttt{ab49ce9}, and it \emph{is} present in this fork's prompts.

And the deepest one, \texttt{LEARNINGS} \S2: a null model using \textbf{only the timestamp},
with zero pixel access, beat \texttt{p\_hit} on 3 of 4 tasks; \texttt{p\_hit} correlated with
video time at $|r|$ up to \textbf{0.93}. In the repo's own words: \emph{``The system was
scoring worse than a clock.''}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Conclusion.} 27 videos against our 2{,}700, a judge that was never running, and a
control signal that was largely a clock. The 0.206 is not a target to chase. What \emph{is}
real in that branch are its \textbf{mechanisms}: hysteresis with re-arm (\texttt{hyst2b}),
the per-tick \texttt{seen} step, word-overlap dedup within a true stretch, and the
``already reported'' history. Those survive the audit; the score does not.
\end{keybox}

\noindent\textbf{The omni fork wins on 2 of 9 tasks.} Micro-averaged it trails by 0.022 ---
inside the 0.03 noise band --- but \textbf{macro-averaged it trails by 0.064}, which is not.
The micro figure flatters us because it is dominated by the tasks we do adequately; the
macro figure exposes that our three worst tasks are much worse.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{The two systems fail in opposite directions.} The parent emits
\textbf{1.48} times per event at precision 0.225 / recall 0.333; we emit \textbf{3.45} times
at precision 0.159 / recall 0.548. It is conservative and accurate; we are talkative and
vague. Our advantage on \texttt{event\_narration} and \texttt{realtime\_state\_monitor} --- both
\texttt{level}-mode, short-refractory tasks --- is exactly where talking more pays.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{Where we lose most, the parent simply gets more attempts.} On the three
group-B tasks the parent emits 2.85 / 3.07 / 1.33 times per event where our inherited
refractories permit 1.00 / 0.95 / 0.76.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Read this table with four caveats.} (i) \emph{Different subsets} --- the parent's
$n$ is uneven and small on exactly the tasks where it wins most
(\texttt{explicit\_target\_grounding} $n{=}12$, \texttt{instant\_event\_alert} $n{=}38$); ours is
$\approx$280 per task. Those two rows are the weakest in the table. (ii) \emph{Different
modality and backbone} --- this is not an ablation of audio, it is two different systems.
(iii) \emph{Content is not comparable at all}: the parent ran with a working judge
(\texttt{n\_unjudged}=0), ours is \textsc{withheld}. Only time-F1 is being compared, and only
because the $\pm$3\,s greedy match is the same code path in both trees. (iv) The parent's
numbers are read from another account's scratch, which this project has already been burned
by once; they should be re-derived before publication.
\end{keybox}

\section{What to do, in priority order}

Ordered by (evidence it will help) $\times$ (cheapness), not by ambition.

\subsection*{Tier 1 --- attacks the confirmed root cause}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Constrain the decode to the schema.} The model stops mid-JSON at 22 tokens.
Guided/constrained decoding (a grammar or a logit-processor mask over the schema) makes the
whole DSL structurally unskippable. This alone restores \texttt{answer} on the 49\% of ticks
that currently lack it and \texttt{event\_time\_s} on the 59\% that lack it.
\emph{Cost: hours. Expected effect: large and broad.}

\item \textbf{Shrink the schema for this model.} Qwen2.5-Omni-7B is a weaker structured
generator than Qwen3-VL-8B. Two fields (\texttt{have\_enough\_info},
\texttt{event\_time\_s}) may outperform seven that it never finishes. Test the reduced
schema against the full one at equal budget. \emph{Cost: hours.}

\item \textbf{Use \texttt{event\_time\_s} --- the gate currently ignores it.} The model
already names an onset time on 41\% of ticks, and the emission is timestamped at the
\emph{crossing tick} instead. Emitting at the model's stated time is a different, possibly
much better, timing signal that costs \textbf{no GPU} to evaluate: it is already in the
banked logs. \emph{Do this first --- it is free.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Tier 2 --- cheap, plausible, unproven}

\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item \textbf{Add the four modality delimiters} (\S3.3) and A/B one task. Cheap, and it
restores a training-time invariant we are currently violating.

\item \textbf{Detrend \texttt{p\_hit} within each video.} The parent measured $|r|$ up to
0.93 between \texttt{p\_hit} and wall-clock. If that holds here, most of the score is a
clock; z-scoring or differencing within a video could expose the residual. \textbf{Free} ---
offline, from banked tick logs.

\item \textbf{Gate on the derivative, not the level.} Events are \emph{changes}. A
rising-edge gate on a level signal fires wherever the level is high. Also free to test
offline.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Tier 3 --- known-bounded gains}

\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item \textbf{Refit mode and refractory} (never refitted; inherited from the vision-only
model). \emph{Promoted after the \S5 comparison}: the parent emits 2.85$\times$ per event on
\texttt{snapshot\_counting} and 3.07$\times$ on \texttt{explicit\_target\_grounding} where our
600\,s / 300\,s refractories permit 1.00, and it scores 0.28 and 0.23 against our 0.07 and
0.11. This is worth more than the earlier ceiling analysis suggested --- it binds on all
three group-B tasks, which are our three worst.

\item \textbf{Do not chase the emission budget.} Matching emissions to event count would
\emph{reduce} $F_1$ while precision stays flat. Revisit only after Tier 1 makes the score
informative.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Content-F1 specifically}

\begin{enumerate}[resume]
\item \textbf{Restore a judge.} \texttt{content\_acc} and \texttt{joint\_f1} are
\textsc{withheld} --- never guessed --- and will stay so until one is reachable. The true
value lies between the lower bound 0.038 and the timing ceiling 0.246; nothing narrows that
without a judge. This blocks any comparison with MiniCPM-o 4.5 (0.209 \emph{joint}-F1, not
time-F1, and judged by a different model on a different subset).

\item \textbf{Fix \texttt{answer} degeneracy} --- same root cause as item 1. An
\texttt{answer} of \texttt{"\{"} scores zero content no matter how good the timing is, so
\emph{every} joint-F1 point is currently gated behind Tier 1.

\item \textbf{Emit the benchmark's token form, not prose}, per task. Already done for
\texttt{realtime\_state\_monitor}; audit the remaining constrained-format tasks the same
way.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The honest bottom line}

\begin{keybox}
The threshold-fit study's negative result stands and is well-evidenced: \emph{given this
control signal}, no per-task threshold recovers information the signal does not carry, and
the fit does not overfit (fit-disjoint bias $+0.0007$ gross). What the study could not see
from inside is that \textbf{the signal itself is the artefact of a broken generation step}.
The right next move is not a better gate. It is to make the controller finish its sentence.
\end{keybox}

\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent\small\textbf{Two caveats on this document.} (i) The DSL-collapse diagnosis is
measured; the claim that fixing it raises $F_1$ is a \emph{hypothesis}, and item 3 is the
free experiment that tests it before any GPU is spent. (ii) The audit of the parent branch's
0.206 is read from this repository's own post-mortems; that branch has not been re-run here.
