\large Qwen2.5-Omni-7B on OmniPro Online --- the full record:\\
approach, results, figures, findings, and what none of it shows}
\author{}
\date{1 September 2026}

\vspace{-2.4em}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{One-paragraph summary.} A streaming assistant must decide \emph{when} to
speak. It decides by thresholding $p_{hit}$, a probability read off the frozen
model's logits. This study fitted that threshold \emph{per task} over two grid
passes on a frozen 135-video subset, then ran the complete 2{,}700-sample
benchmark, which is now \textbf{finished: 2{,}700/2{,}700, gross time-F1 0.2265}.
\textbf{The fit does not hold up, and the reason is measurable}: across
all nine tasks the AUC of $p_{hit}$ against event-adjacency is \textbf{0.394--0.526}
--- chance --- so a threshold controls \emph{how often} the system speaks and never
\emph{when}. Nine fitted thresholds do not beat one global threshold outside noise.
The negative result is established four independent ways and is the contribution.
\end{keybox}

\section{What the system does and what is being fitted}

A video plays. The model watches and listens as it arrives. At some moment it
should \emph{speak} --- report a count, name a state change, alert on an event. The
question is not \emph{what} to say; it is \textbf{when}.

The deciding machinery is small. Once per tick the system asks the frozen model a
yes/no question --- ``do we have enough information / has the event happened?'' ---
and reads $P(\texttt{true})$ off the logits. Call it $p_{hit}$. If $p_{hit}$ crosses
a threshold, the system speaks.

Nine tasks share that mechanism, and the motivating guess was that they want
\emph{different} thresholds: a task that should alert once per video is not a task
that should narrate continuously.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The benchmark.} OmniPro Online: 9 tasks $\times$ 300 videos $=$
\textbf{2{,}700} samples, 24.1\,s--594.6\,s, 65\% requiring audio.
\textbf{The metric.} \texttt{time\_f1}: an emission counts if it falls within
$\pm$3\,s of a ground-truth trigger, matched greedily one-to-one. It needs no LLM
judge, which is why every decision here is made on it.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The gate is three coupled knobs, not one:} the threshold, the mode
(\texttt{edge} = fire on a rising crossing, \texttt{level} = fire on every tick
above), and the refractory (seconds of enforced silence after firing). \textbf{Only
the threshold was refitted.} The other two are inherited unchanged from the
vision-only predecessor. This is the study's largest structural limitation and it
recurs throughout.

\section{Why the fit was necessary: the knob was disconnected}

An audit of the shipped system found the threshold was not in the firing path at
all. Under \texttt{gate\_strategy="hysteresis"} the test that actually decided
firing was \texttt{p\_hit >= gate\_high\_thr}, a fixed global \textbf{0.5}; the
per-task \texttt{hit\_threshold} only decided whether an answer was \emph{decoded}.

Across a completed 2{,}700-sample run --- \textbf{581{,}403 ticks, 50{,}433 fires}
--- \textbf{not one fire occurred below $p_{hit}=0.500$}. The four tasks configured
at or below 0.5 were all silently running at 0.5. \texttt{task\_gate\_modes} and
\texttt{task\_refractory\_s} were read \textbf{nowhere at all}.

\begin{keybox}
The failure was \textbf{silent}: it produced a finished run and a number you could
put in a table. It also means the ``shipped'' thresholds were never actually tested
at their stated values --- so this study's baseline is itself a fiction that had to
be re-measured.
\end{keybox}

\subsection{A second defect, found while the fit was running}

$p_{hit}$ is high on the \textbf{very first tick} of every video: the model answers
from the prompt before it has seen anything ($\ge$0.05 for 98--100\% of samples,
median 0.34--0.64). Seeding the gate's \texttt{prev\_above} to \texttt{False} made
that first tick a rising edge, so a long-refractory task spent its single allowed
emission at $t\approx1$\,s against ground truth at 26--98\,s.

The fix is definitional --- an edge requires a predecessor, so \texttt{prev\_above}
starts \texttt{None}. Offline best time-F1 rose $0.0535\rightarrow0.0900$
(\texttt{snapshot\_counting}) and $0.0636\rightarrow0.0751$
(\texttt{instant\_event\_alert}); short-refractory tasks moved $\pm$0.001.
\textbf{The size of the effect tracks the refractory}, which is the practical proof
that the three knobs cannot honestly be fitted one at a time.

\section{Approach}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llrrl}
\toprule
stage & what it does & cells & sample-evals & status \\
\midrule
pass 1  & wide grid, 10 thresholds $\times$ 9 tasks & 94 & 1{,}410 & complete \\
pass 2  & refine 5 points around each pass-1 winner & 45 &   675 & complete \\
stage 3 & the full benchmark at the fitted values   &  1 & 2{,}700 & \textbf{complete} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{The frozen subset.} Passes 1--2 run on a fixed 5\% all-audio subset
--- \textbf{15 videos per task, 135 total} --- chosen once and never changed.
Freezing it is what makes the two passes comparable to each other and to the
bootstrap auditing them.

\noindent\textbf{Protocol rules behind every number here:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bootstrap resamples \emph{videos}}, never ticks or events. Ticks within
a video are serially correlated through one shared KV cache; resampling them would
manufacture confidence that is not there.
\item \textbf{Paired, and it replays the selection rule.} Every threshold is scored
on the \emph{same} resampled video set, and the rule is re-run inside each draw
rather than an argmax taken --- so the audit measures the stability of \emph{the
procedure}, not of one number.
\item \textbf{Re-selection is always divided by chance.} A 10-cell grid has a 10\%
chance rate; a 5-cell grid, 20\%. Comparing raw rates across grid sizes is
meaningless, and doing so would have made pass 2 look better than pass 1.
\item \textbf{A 0.03 noise band.} The eval is not bit-reproducible; run-to-run
variance on one fixed config was measured at F1 0.255 vs 0.051.
\item \textbf{Content metrics are \textsc{withheld}, never estimated} when the judge
is unreachable. A blank cell is a fact; a guessed cell is a lie that reaches a paper.
\end{itemize}

\section{Findings}

\subsection{Finding 1 --- \texorpdfstring{$p_{hit}$}{p\_hit} does not rank event-adjacent ticks above quiet ones}

This governs everything else. The ROC of per-tick $p_{hit}$ against a $\pm$3\,s
positive label, now measured on \textbf{all nine tasks} with paired-over-video
bootstrap CIs:

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\\[2pt]
{\footnotesize $^{\dagger}$ the only CI that excludes 0.5 --- and it excludes it on the \emph{wrong side}.}
\end{center}

\textbf{0.5 is chance}, and eight of the nine CIs contain it. The obvious escapes
were all checked and closed: not a clock offset (swept $\pm$10\,s --- no peak), not
too tight a tolerance ($\pm$10\,s --- unchanged), not a degenerate score (232--284
distinct values spanning 0.000--0.997).

\begin{keybox}
Two details worth pausing on. \textbf{(i) Eight of nine AUCs are \emph{below} 0.5.}
Pure noise would scatter symmetrically about 0.5; a median of 0.455 is a systematic
tilt, consistent with the predecessor's finding that $p_{hit}$ correlates with
wall-clock time ($|r|$ up to 0.93) rather than with content. \textbf{(ii)
\texttt{dedup\_counting}'s CI excludes 0.5 on the wrong side} --- its $p_{hit}$ is
significantly \emph{anti}-correlated with events. That is worse than uninformative:
thresholding it actively selects the wrong moments.
\end{keybox}

\subsection{Finding 2 --- no task's fitted threshold survives its own bootstrap}

Pass 1 completed (1{,}410/1{,}410, 94/94 cells) and the rule returned a winner for
all nine tasks. The paired bootstrap then found:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{9 of 9} tasks have a $\Delta$F1-against-best-rival CI \textbf{containing
zero}.
\item Re-selection runs 25--61\% against an 8--10\% chance rate ---
\texttt{semantic\_condition\_alert} at just \textbf{6\%}, i.e.\ \emph{below} chance.
\item Two ``fits'' are artefacts: \texttt{instant\_event\_alert} scores 0.000 at
every threshold, and \texttt{explicit\_target\_grounding}'s entire curve is one
matched event or zero out of 16.
\end{itemize}

\begin{figure}[t]\centering\fig{fig7_bootstrap_forest.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Findings 2 and 4 in one picture.} Each task's $\Delta$time-F1 of the
fitted threshold against its best rival, with 95\% paired-bootstrap CI, for both

\subsection{Finding 3 --- the gate has one identifiable degree of freedom, not nine}

All arms scored on the same videos at equal budget:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrl}
\toprule
arm & time-P & time-R & \textbf{time-F1} & vs.\ best single global \\
\midrule
\texttt{shipped\_per\_task}          & 0.151 & 0.356 & 0.2115 & $-$0.0354, CI $[-0.055,-0.016]$ \\
\texttt{global\_0.5}                 & 0.142 & 0.565 & 0.2271 & $-$0.0198, CI $[-0.039,-0.003]$ \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Nine fitted thresholds beat one global threshold by \textbf{+0.0075 with a CI
straddling zero} --- \emph{in sample}, where the nine have nine free parameters to
its one. Yet the single global value is \emph{solid}: re-running the sweep inside
every bootstrap draw, \textbf{0.15 is re-selected in 94\%} of them against 10\%
chance.

\begin{keybox}
Pooled over 135 videos the operating point is identified; split nine ways over 15
videos each it is not. \textbf{That is a power problem, not a claim that the tasks
are alike.} Note also that the \emph{shipped} per-task thresholds are significantly
\emph{worse} than a flat 0.15 ($-$0.0354), and the damage is in recall (0.356 vs
0.601) --- they were strangling the system. Replacing them is a real improvement
even though the per-task \emph{fit} is not.
\end{keybox}

\subsection{Finding 4 --- pass 2 confirmed a prediction registered before the data existed}

Before pass 2 finished, a prediction was written down: the refined grid's CIs would
contain zero on \emph{at least seven of nine} tasks. \textbf{The outcome was 9 of 9.}

The whole refined F1 span is 0.021--0.133, and on four tasks \emph{every} cell sits
within the noise band of the best. Two honesty notes are carried in the record: two
rows report CI $[+0.000,+0.200]$ whose lower bound is \emph{exactly} zero and
therefore does not exclude it; and pass 2's re-selection of 38--74\% beats pass 1's
25--61\% only until you divide by chance (20\% vs 8--10\%) --- \textbf{as a multiple
of chance, pass 2 is no better}.

\subsection{Finding 5 --- the fit does not overfit}

Stage 3's 2{,}700 samples \emph{contain} the 135 fitting videos, making the headline
$\approx$5\% in-sample. Rescoring with those ids removed --- free, the same
predictions filtered --- gives an in-sample bias of \textbf{+0.0003 gross} on the
completed run (2{,}565 disjoint samples), and $+$0.0003 in both audio strata
separately: two orders of magnitude inside the noise band. Whatever else is true of this fit, it
generalises. The negative result is about \emph{identifiability}, not overfitting.

\subsection{Two failure regimes, not one}

\textbf{Regime A --- over-emission (six tasks).} 2.6--6.1 emissions per event.
Precision, not recall, is what limits these. Gross precision is 0.143 against recall
0.546: the system finds the moments and buries them in false positives.

\textbf{Regime B --- one emission per video (three tasks).} These carry 300\,s and
600\,s refractories inherited from the vision-only fit.
\texttt{snapshot\_counting} is the clean case: exactly 300 ground-truth events over
300 videos, exactly 300 emissions, and F1 \textbf{0.0600} --- so the single emission
lands within $\pm$3\,s exactly 6\% of the time. The \emph{count} is perfect; the
\emph{moment} is wrong. \texttt{explicit\_target\_grounding} is the same shape at
1.00 and 0.0994.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{The gate is nearly exhausted, and the intuitive fix is backwards.} With
precision flat across the grid, $F_1 = 2pr/(p+r)$ rises monotonically as $r\to1$ to a
ceiling of $2p/(p{+}1) = \mathbf{0.250}$. We sit at \textbf{0.2265}, so the entire
remaining headroom in the gate is \textbf{0.024} --- inside the 0.03 noise band ---
and \emph{cutting} the over-emission would \textbf{lower} F1, because precision will
not rise to compensate.
\end{keybox}

\emph{and} we take a third as many attempts.

\section{Limitations --- read before quoting any number}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Only one of three coupled knobs was refitted.} \texttt{mode} and
\texttt{refractory\_s} are inherited unchanged. The evidence above says they are
coupled to the threshold, and \S6.1 shows the cost concretely: the 300\,s/600\,s
refractories permit $\approx$1 emission per video on the three worst tasks.

\item \textbf{There is no out-of-sample nine-versus-one comparison.} A control arm
(flat global 0.15 over all 2{,}700) was built, preflighted, then \textbf{cancelled
before launch}. The only fitted-vs-global number is the in-sample $+0.0075$, and it
must always be cited as \emph{in sample, $n{=}135$, nine free parameters against
one}. Two predictions registered for that arm are \textbf{withdrawn, not resolved}.

\item \textbf{15 videos per cell is underpowered}, and that is the direct cause of
Findings 2--4 rather than an incidental weakness.

\item \textbf{\texttt{content\_acc} and \texttt{joint\_f1} are \textsc{withheld}}
throughout. No LLM judge has produced a verdict in any run of this fork: the workers
\texttt{unset} the API keys deliberately, and the configured model
(\texttt{gemini-3-flash}) 404s when tried. Seven of nine tasks are content-scored by
deterministic parsers (count, position, state, or \texttt{time\_only}); the two
free-text tasks are unjudged, so the \emph{pooled} figure cannot be formed. On the
completed run the true joint-F1 lies between the lower bound \textbf{0.0349} and the
timing ceiling \textbf{0.2265}, at 57\% judge coverage. No
comparison with published baselines (MiniCPM-o 4.5 at 0.209 \emph{joint}-F1) is
available --- and they used a different judge on a different subset.

\item \textbf{The eval is not bit-reproducible.} Treat $<$0.03 as noise, always.

\item \textbf{The deepest one.} A separate diagnosis found the controller's ICL
schema \emph{collapsing} on this backbone: a mean of \textbf{21.7 generated tokens
against a 300-token budget}, with the self-pacing fields appearing in 20 ticks out of
78{,}828 and \texttt{answer} present on only 51\%. The boolean this entire study
thresholds is produced by that generation step. So Finding 1's AUC $\approx0.5$ is
plausibly \textbf{an artefact of a broken upstream stage rather than a property of
the task}. The findings remain correct \emph{about the system as it ran}; what they
predict about a repaired system is an open question.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions, and what to do next}

\begin{keybox}
Per-task threshold fitting did not work, and this study establishes \emph{why}: not
because the tasks are alike, but because the signal being thresholded carries no
timing information, and because 15 videos per task cannot identify nine parameters.
A single global threshold (0.15) is the only operating point the data identifies,
and it is identified well (94\% re-selection against 10\% chance).
\end{keybox}

\noindent\textbf{Worth carrying forward:}
\begin{itemize}
\item A negative result established by \textbf{four independent code paths} --- ROC,
flat precision curve, paired bootstrap, refined grid --- with predictions registered
before the data and \emph{withdrawals recorded as visibly as confirmations}.
\item The fit \textbf{generalises} ($+$0.0007 in-sample bias).
\item The shipped thresholds are \textbf{measurably harmful} versus a flat 0.15.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{The next move is not a better gate} --- with precision flat there is
$\le$0.03 left in it. Ranked by evidence:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Use \texttt{event\_time\_s}, which is free.} The model already names an
onset time on 41\% of ticks and the gate discards it, timestamping emissions at the
crossing tick instead. The data is already banked; testing costs no GPU.
\item \textbf{Make the controller finish its schema} (constrained decoding, or a
smaller schema this backbone can complete).
\item \textbf{Refit mode and refractory}, which the head-to-head shows is worth more
than the ceiling analysis alone suggested --- it binds on the three worst tasks.
\item \textbf{Restore a judge.} Every content number is blocked behind it.
\end{enumerate}
