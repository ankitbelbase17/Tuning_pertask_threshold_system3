\large Qwen2.5-Omni-7B on OmniPro Online --- what it is, what it found,\\
and what it does not show}
\author{}
\date{1 September 2026}

\section{The question, in one page}

The system is a \textbf{proactive streaming assistant}. A video plays; the model
watches and listens as it arrives; at some moment it should \emph{speak} --- report
a count, name a state change, alert on an event. The research question is not
\emph{what} to say. It is \textbf{when}.

The machinery for deciding is small. Once per tick the system asks the frozen model
a yes/no question --- ``do we have enough information / has the event happened?''
--- and reads the probability of \texttt{true} straight off the logits. Call it
$p_{hit}$. If $p_{hit}$ crosses a threshold, the system speaks.

That threshold is the object of this study. There are nine tasks, and the guess
motivating the work was that they want \emph{different} thresholds: a task that
should alert once per video is not a task that should narrate continuously.

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{So the study asks:} does fitting one threshold \emph{per task} produce a
better streaming system than a single threshold shared by all nine? And the answer
turns out to be more interesting than a yes or a no.
\end{keybox}

\noindent\textbf{The benchmark} is OmniPro Online: 9 tasks $\times$ 300 videos =
\textbf{2{,}700} samples, 24.1\,s to 594.6\,s long, 65\% of them requiring audio.
\textbf{The metric} is \texttt{time\_f1}: an emission counts if it lands within
$\pm$3\,s of a ground-truth trigger, matched greedily one-to-one. It needs no LLM
judge, which is why every decision in this study is made on it.

\section{Why it had to be done at all: the threshold was doing nothing}

Before any fitting, an audit of the shipped system found the knob was
\textbf{disconnected}.

Under the shipped \texttt{gate\_strategy="hysteresis"}, the test that actually
decides whether to fire was \texttt{p\_hit >= gate\_high\_thr} --- a fixed global
\textbf{0.5}. The per-task \texttt{hit\_threshold} only decided whether an answer got
\emph{decoded}. Across a completed 2{,}700-sample run (581{,}403 ticks, 50{,}433
fires) \textbf{not one fire occurred below $p_{hit}=0.500$}. The four tasks
configured at or under 0.5 were all silently running at 0.5. Two further
parameters, \texttt{task\_gate\_modes} and \texttt{task\_refractory\_s}, were read
\textbf{nowhere at all}.

\begin{keybox}
This is the pattern the whole project keeps meeting: \textbf{the failure was silent}.
It produced a finished run and a number you could put in a table. Fixing the gate so
the threshold is genuinely in the firing path is what made a fit meaningful --- and
it means the ``shipped'' thresholds were never really tested at their stated values.
\end{keybox}

\noindent A second defect surfaced while the fit was already running. $p_{hit}$ is
\emph{high on the very first tick} of every video --- the model answers the question
from the prompt before it has seen anything ($\ge$0.05 for 98--100\% of samples,
median 0.34--0.64). Seeding the gate's \texttt{prev\_above} to \texttt{False} made
that first tick a rising edge, so a task with a long refractory spent its single
allowed emission at $t\approx1$\,s against ground truth at 26--98\,s. The fix is
definitional --- an edge requires a predecessor, so \texttt{prev\_above} starts
\texttt{None}. Offline best time-F1 rose 0.0535\,$\rightarrow$\,0.0900
(\texttt{snapshot\_counting}) and 0.0636\,$\rightarrow$\,0.0751
(\texttt{instant\_event\_alert}); short-refractory tasks moved by $\pm$0.001.

\noindent\textbf{The size of that effect tracks the refractory} --- which is the
practical reason the gate's three knobs (\texttt{threshold}, \texttt{mode},
\texttt{refractory}) cannot honestly be fitted one at a time.

\section{The approach}

Three stages, deliberately cheap before expensive.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llrrl}
\toprule
stage & what it does & cells & sample-evals & status \\
\midrule
pass 1 & wide grid, 10 thresholds $\times$ 9 tasks & 94 & 1{,}410 & complete \\
pass 2 & refine 5 points around each pass-1 winner & 45 & 675 & complete \\
stage 3 & full benchmark at the fitted values & 1 & 2{,}700 & 94\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{The frozen subset.} Passes 1 and 2 run on a fixed 5\% all-audio
subset --- \textbf{15 videos per task, 135 in total} --- chosen once and never
changed. Freezing it is what makes the two passes comparable to each other and to
the bootstrap that audits them.

\noindent\textbf{Protocol rules that shaped every number here:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The bootstrap resamples \emph{videos}, never ticks or events.} Ticks
within one video are serially correlated through a shared KV cache; resampling them
would manufacture confidence that is not there.
\item \textbf{Paired.} Every threshold is scored on the \emph{same} resampled video
set, and the selection rule is re-run inside each draw rather than an argmax being
taken --- so the audit measures the stability of \emph{the procedure}, not of a
number.
\item \textbf{Re-selection is always divided by chance.} A grid of 10 cells has a
10\% chance rate, one of 5 cells has 20\%. Comparing raw re-selection rates across
grids of different size is meaningless.
\item \textbf{A 0.03 noise band.} The eval is not bit-reproducible; run-to-run
variance on one fixed config has been measured at F1 0.255 vs 0.051. Differences
below 0.03 are not treated as real.
\item \textbf{Content metrics are \textsc{withheld}, never estimated}, whenever the
LLM judge is unreachable. A blank cell is a fact; a guessed cell is a lie that
survives into a paper.
\end{itemize}

\section{The findings, in the order they arrived}

\subsection{Finding 1 --- $p_{hit}$ does not rank event-adjacent ticks above quiet ones}

This is the central result. On the five tasks with a complete reference cell, the
ROC of per-tick $p_{hit}$ against a $\pm$3\,s positive label:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
task & ticks & distinct $p_{hit}$ & \textbf{AUC} \\
\midrule
semantic\_condition\_alert  & 3{,}323 & 253 & \textbf{0.431} \\
cumulative\_counting        & 2{,}833 & 284 & \textbf{0.469} \\
explicit\_target\_grounding & 2{,}947 & 232 & \textbf{0.512} \\
snapshot\_counting          & 3{,}703 & 280 & \textbf{0.526} \\
instant\_event\_alert       & 4{,}047 & 247 & \textbf{0.551} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{0.5 is chance.} Every 95\% CI contains it. And the obvious escapes were all
checked and closed: it is not a clock offset (swept $\pm$10\,s --- no peak), not too
tight a tolerance ($\pm$10\,s --- unchanged), not a degenerate score (232--284
distinct values spanning 0.000--0.997).

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Why this governs everything else.} A threshold is a \emph{ranking} device: it
works only if the score is higher near events than away from them. If AUC is 0.5, the
threshold controls \emph{how often} the system speaks, never \emph{when}. Every
result below is this fact arriving again by a different route.
\end{keybox}

\subsection{Finding 2 --- no task's fitted threshold survives its own bootstrap}

Pass 1 completed (1{,}410/1{,}410, 94/94 cells) and the rule returned a winner for
all nine tasks. Then the paired bootstrap:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{9 of 9} tasks have a $\Delta$F1-against-best-rival CI \textbf{containing
zero}.
\item Re-selection is 25--61\% against an 8--10\% chance rate ---
\texttt{semantic\_condition\_alert} at just \textbf{6\%}, i.e.\ \emph{below} chance.
\item Two ``fits'' are artefacts: \texttt{instant\_event\_alert} scores 0.000
everywhere, and \texttt{explicit\_target\_grounding}'s entire curve is one matched
event or zero out of 16.
\end{itemize}

The only reproducible structure in the whole grid is the collapse at 0.95 --- i.e.\
the threshold controls emission \emph{volume}, exactly as an AUC of 0.5 predicts.

\subsection{Finding 3 --- the gate has one identifiable degree of freedom, not nine}

A fair arena, all arms scored on the same videos at equal budget:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrl}
\toprule
arm & time-P & time-R & \textbf{time-F1} & vs.\ best single global \\
\midrule
\texttt{shipped\_per\_task}     & 0.151 & 0.356 & 0.2115 & $-$0.0354, CI $[-0.055,-0.016]$ \\
\texttt{global\_0.5}            & 0.142 & 0.565 & 0.2271 & $-$0.0198, CI $[-0.039,-0.003]$ \\
\texttt{best\_single\_global} (0.15) & 0.155 & 0.601 & 0.2469 & --- \\
\texttt{fitted\_per\_task}      & 0.163 & 0.582 & \textbf{0.2544} & $+$0.0075, CI $[-0.011,+0.027]$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Nine fitted thresholds beat one global threshold by \textbf{+0.0075 with a
CI straddling zero} --- \emph{in sample}, where the nine have nine free parameters to
its one. Yet the single global value is \emph{solid}: re-running the sweep inside
every bootstrap draw, \textbf{0.15 is re-selected in 94\%} of them against a 10\%
chance rate.

\begin{keybox}
Pooled over 135 videos the operating point is identified. Split nine ways over 15
videos each, it is not. \textbf{That is a power problem, not a claim that the nine
tasks are alike.} Note also that the \emph{shipped} per-task thresholds are
significantly \emph{worse} than a flat 0.15 ($-$0.0354), and the damage is in recall
(0.356 vs 0.601): they were strangling the system.
\end{keybox}

\subsection{Finding 4 --- pass 2 confirmed a prediction made before the data existed}

Before pass 2 finished, a prediction was registered in writing: the refined grid's
CIs would contain zero on \emph{at least seven of nine} tasks.

\textbf{The outcome was 9 of 9.} Across each refined neighbourhood the entire F1 span
is 0.021--0.133, and on four tasks \emph{every} cell sits within the 0.03 noise band
of the best. Refining did not resolve a winner; it confirmed there was no gradient to
resolve.

Two honesty notes carried in the record: two rows report CI $[+0.000,+0.200]$ whose
lower bound is \emph{exactly} zero and therefore does not exclude it (those tasks
match 0--2 events out of 15--16); and pass 2's re-selection of 38--74\% looks better
than pass 1's 25--61\% only until you divide by chance --- 20\% here against 8--10\%
there. \textbf{As a multiple of chance, pass 2 is no better.}

\subsection{Finding 5 --- the fit does not overfit}

Stage 3's 2{,}700 samples \emph{contain} the 135 fitting videos, so the headline is
$\approx$5\% in-sample. Rescoring with those ids removed --- free, the same
predictions filtered --- gives an in-sample bias of \textbf{+0.0007 gross}, two orders
of magnitude inside the noise band. Whatever else is true of this fit, it generalises.

\section{Stage 3: the full benchmark}

At 94\% banked (2{,}553 of 2{,}700), judge offline:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
stratum & $n$ & $n_{gt}$ & $n_{emit}$ & t-P / t-R & \textbf{time-F1} \\
\midrule
audio required     & 1643 & 4916 & 16185 & 0.155 / 0.510 & 0.2375 \\
audio not required &  868 & 3511 & 12866 & 0.164 / 0.601 & 0.2578 \\
\textbf{gross}     & 2511 & 8427 & 29051 & 0.159 / 0.548 & \textbf{0.2464} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The system emits \textbf{3.4$\times$ more often than there are events}.
Precision, not recall, is what limits the score. And because precision is flat across
the whole grid, the gate's absolute ceiling is $2p/(p{+}1)=\mathbf{0.274}$: every
remaining threshold adjustment in existence is worth $\le$0.028, i.e.\ inside the
noise band.

\subsection{Against the vision-only parent}

The parent system (Qwen3-VL-8B, vision only, its own fitted thresholds, and a
\emph{working} judge) versus ours, on time-F1 only:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
 & emits per event & micro time-F1 & macro time-F1 \\
\midrule
system\_3 (Qwen3-VL-8B, V), $n{=}931$   & 1.48 & \textbf{0.2684} & \textbf{0.2565} \\
omni (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, A+V), $n{=}2511$ & 3.45 & \textbf{0.2464} & \textbf{0.1927} \\

\noindent We win on 2 of 9 tasks. Micro-averaged we trail by 0.022 (inside the noise
band); \textbf{macro-averaged by 0.064}, which is not. The micro figure flatters us
because it is dominated by the tasks we do adequately. The parent is conservative and
accurate; we are talkative and vague. \emph{Caveat:} different subsets, different
backbone, different modality --- and the parent's $n$ is as low as 12 on a task where
it beats us most. This is a signpost, not a controlled comparison.

\section{Limitations --- read this section before quoting any number}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Only one of the gate's three knobs was refitted.} \texttt{mode} and
\texttt{refractory\_s} are inherited unchanged from the vision-only model. The
document's own evidence says they are coupled to the threshold, so this bounds what
any threshold result can mean. It shows up concretely: the 600\,s / 300\,s
refractories permit $\approx$1 emission per video on three tasks, and those are our
three worst.

\item \textbf{There is no out-of-sample nine-vs-one comparison.} A control arm (flat
global 0.15 over all 2{,}700) was built, preflighted, and then \textbf{cancelled
before launch} on 1 September 2026. The only fitted-vs-global number is the in-sample
$+0.0075$ above, and it must always be cited as \emph{in sample, $n{=}135$, nine free
parameters against one}. Two predictions registered for that arm are
\textbf{withdrawn, not resolved}.

\item \textbf{15 videos per cell is underpowered}, and the study says so rather than
working around it. That is the direct cause of Findings 2--4.

\item \textbf{\texttt{content\_acc} and \texttt{joint\_f1} are \textsc{withheld}}
throughout --- the judge is unreachable. The true joint-F1 lies between the lower
bound 0.038 and the timing ceiling 0.246, and nothing narrows that without a judge.
No comparison to published baselines (MiniCPM-o 4.5 at 0.209 \emph{joint}-F1) is
available, and they were judged by a different model on a different subset anyway.

\item \textbf{The eval is not bit-reproducible.} Treat $<$0.03 as noise, always.

\item \textbf{And the deepest one:} a separate diagnosis found the controller's ICL
schema \emph{collapsing} on this backbone --- a mean of 21.7 generated tokens against
a 300-token budget, with the self-pacing fields appearing in 20 ticks out of 78{,}828.
The boolean this study thresholds is produced by that generation step. So Finding 1's
AUC $\approx$ 0.5 is very plausibly \textbf{an artefact of a broken upstream stage
rather than a property of the task}, which would mean this study measured the gate of
a system that was not perceiving properly. That does not invalidate the findings ---
they are correct \emph{about the system as it ran} --- but it changes what they
predict about a repaired system.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What it all means}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{The honest summary.} Per-task threshold fitting did not work, and the study
is strong evidence about \emph{why}: not because the tasks are alike, but because the
signal being thresholded carries no timing information, and because 15 videos per
task cannot identify nine parameters. A single global threshold (0.15) is the only
operating point this data identifies, and it is identified well. The shipped per-task
thresholds are measurably worse than that single value, so replacing them is a real
improvement even though the per-task \emph{fit} is not.
\end{keybox}

\noindent\textbf{What is worth carrying forward:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A negative result, properly established} --- by four independent code
paths (ROC, flat precision curve, paired bootstrap, refined grid), with predictions
registered before the data and \emph{withdrawals recorded as visibly as hits}.
\item \textbf{The fit generalises} (+0.0007 in-sample bias), so the negative result is
about identifiability, not overfitting.
\item \textbf{The next move is not a better gate.} With precision flat, the gate has
$\le$0.028 left in it. The gains are upstream: make the controller finish its schema,
and use the onset time the model already names on 41\% of ticks and the gate currently
throws away.
\end{itemize}
